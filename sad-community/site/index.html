Does social media pressure turn into negative emotions?

Research suggests that social media itself is not the whole problem. Most of the emotional harm happens when constant checking and comparing turn into fear of missing out (FoMO).
Source: Psychology Research and Behavior Management (Chen et al., 2023)

Hover over each circle to see what the data means

85% via FoMO
FoMO pathway About 85% of the emotional harm linked to social media is carried through fear of missing out — constant comparison and worrying about being left out.
Strong effect
FoMO → emotions Higher FoMO strongly predicts anxiety, stress, and low mood — especially for people with lower resilience.
Weak effect
Direct effect Once FoMO is considered, social media use alone shows only a small direct link to negative emotions.

Social media pressure often becomes harmful not because you are online, but because it triggers FoMO. Thus, Harborly is here to reduce comparison, slow down the pace, and help you reconnect in a calmer way.

Who are SADs ?

Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD) affects how people think, feel and connect with others.
Many experience intense worry about being judged, which can make everyday situations — speaking up, meeting new people, or even asking for help — feel overwhelming. At Harborly, we support anyone who feels anxious in social settings and wants a calm, understanding space to grow at their own pace.
