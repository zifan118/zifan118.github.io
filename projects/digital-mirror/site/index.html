About this website

Digital Mirror

Digital Mirror is a personal website built around one question: if the platform shapes who you can be, how much of your digital identity is actually yours? It starts with one day in my own digital life, and ends with a pop star who has 293 million followers.

Who this is for

Anyone who uses social media. If you have ever felt slightly different on different apps — more curated, more casual, more performed — and wondered why, you are the reader I had in mind.

Is this ALL of me?

I live on more than one platform at once, and each one quietly asks me to show up differently. On Xiaohongshu I document. On Instagram I curate, mostly by not posting at all. Same person. Two completely different versions of her.

Xiaohongshu

This is where I actually write. Long captions, photos from real days, comments from strangers in Jiangsu who share my references. The algorithm tags me as England. The community still reads me as home.

VS
Instagram

My Instagram is almost empty. No grid, no captions, no trace of a daily life. The profile picture slot sits there loaded with expectation. I chose not to fill it. That silence is its own kind of statement.

Why this happens

Same person.
Two completely different selves.

Neither version is fake. Neither version is the whole picture. The gap between my Xiaohongshu self and my Instagram self isn't a personal quirk — it's something the platforms ask of me before I even open the app.

The platform shapes the self

Every app has its own quiet set of rules about what kinds of expression are even possible inside it. Instagram rewards curation — the grid, the aesthetic, the follower count as social proof. Xiaohongshu rewards documentation — long captions, location tags, a community that expects real life. I didn't choose to be different on each one. The platforms made that choice for me.

And so does the audience

Meaning isn't fixed either. It depends on who is reading, in what space, with what expectations. On Xiaohongshu, my silence would read as odd. On Instagram, it reads as intentional. Same absence. Two completely different meanings. That isn't just a thing about me — it's a thing about how communication works once a platform sits in the middle of it.

If the platform decides how you appear — how much of your digital identity is actually yours?

In our group podcast, we use Bieber's own Instagram and TikTok as a case study: same person, two completely different versions of him. The scale is different. The pattern is the same.
