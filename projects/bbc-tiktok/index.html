Gendered Labour in BBC News TikTok

On a fifteen-second news video, who gets framed as the one in charge, and who as the one who cares?

Module
COMM2910 Communication Research Methods, University of Leeds
Format
Research proposal with a full coding manual and worked examples
Method
Deductive multimodal content analysis, figure-level coding
Case
@BBCNews on TikTok, January–June 2025; target sample of 180 videos

I. The question

How does BBC News construct gendered divisions of labour through the interaction of visual, textual and graphic modes in its TikTok news posts?

  1. Which occupations and tasks are represented as gendered?
  2. How do visual, textual and graphic elements reinforce or challenge these patterns?
  3. Are these multimodal patterns consistent across news categories, or do they vary with topic?

II. The gap

Decades of research show that news authority is gendered: men dominate expert and institutional source roles. But those findings come from instruments built for broadcast transcripts, which code authority from text alone. They cannot see what a close-up, a centred frame or a caption label does to a role.

On TikTok, where a story is compressed into seconds, visual and graphic modes carry much of that work. Someone captioned as an “expert” may be filmed from a distance at the edge of the frame. This study builds a tool to catch exactly that kind of divergence between modes, at the level of the single video.

III. The coding instrument

One video, six stops. Read down.

  1. StopReadsRecords
  2. T1Video classificationper video

    ID, inclusion check, and one of five news categories: Politics, Crime, Health/Science, Entertainment, International Affairs.

  3. T2Figureper figure

    Each human figure in an identifiable occupational or task-based role becomes one observation: gender and screen time.

  4. T3 to T5 read the same figure in three modes.
  5. T3How they are shownvisual

    Shot distance, camera angle, frame position.

  6. T4How they are spoken oftextual

    Attribution type (decision-maker, expert, witness) and whether they speak on camera.

  7. T5How they are labelledgraphic

    On-screen caption: authority role, affective or relational, or name only.

  8. Where the modes disagree, the caption governs: caption>text>visual.
  9. T6Labour categoryjudgement

    Authority-expressive, care-expressive or other/ambiguous, following Hochschild’s distinction between masculinised and feminised emotional labour.

IV. Theory in use

Entman (1993)
Framing: which roles are made salient, and which are left out
Kress & van Leeuwen (2020)
Multimodal social semiotics: how shot, angle and placement build salience
Hochschild (1983)
Authority-oriented versus care-oriented emotional labour, as coding categories
Hall (1997)
When a framing choice recurs across topics, a habit becomes a representational norm

V. Rigour

Reliability

As a single-coder study, reliability is checked as stability over time: ten per cent of the sample is re-coded after at least seven days, with Cohen’s kappa of .80 as the minimum threshold. Videos are collected weekly in a logged-out browser at a fixed time to limit algorithmic personalisation, and every retrieval session is logged.

Limits

The limits are stated plainly: one account over six months, so no generalisation beyond the case; manual scrolling cannot fully remove the feed’s algorithmic filtering; and pre-defined categories may flatten roles that do not fit the authority/care distinction, which the pilot and codebook decision rules are designed to check.
